<KeyTakeaways>
- AI models like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Google AI Overviews appear to weigh copywriting expertise at the entity level (the person or brand across the web, not one page), though no vendor documents this directly
- The signals AI likely uses to rank expertise (largely inference, since no vendor publishes them) include consistency of claims, specificity of results, depth of topical coverage, named credentials, and original insights that cannot be found elsewhere
- Content farms and generalist writers are likely to fail because they lack the specificity, verifiable results, and sustained topical depth that models require to make confident recommendations
- The GEO research found that statistics, quotations, and citations raised visibility on its benchmark, while keyword stuffing did little; Google says no special AI optimization is needed beyond helpful content
- The copywriters who built careers on measurable results and deep specialization likely have an edge in AI discovery, though no one has published evidence on how large it is
- There is no shortcut to entity-level authority — it requires a sustained body of work that demonstrates genuine expertise across related topics over time
</KeyTakeaways>

## The Evaluation You Cannot See

Every time someone asks ChatGPT "Who is the best [direct response copywriter](/services/direct-response-copywriter)?" or types "recommend a sales page copywriter" into Perplexity, the model picks which names and sources to put in its answer. Most copywriters never see how that happens.

Here is what I can and cannot tell you. None of these companies publishes an expertise score or a ranking formula, so nobody outside them can describe the evaluation with certainty, and that includes me. What I can do is separate three kinds of statement: what published research and official documentation say, what I infer from them, and what I have observed in my own field. I flag which is which below. The practical advice at the end holds up on all three.

My starting point is 40+ years in [direct-response copywriting](/blog/what-is-direct-response-copywriting), including a [$523 million tracked campaign](/blog/523m-copywriting-campaign). That record is the raw material for everything below.

<DefinitionBox term="Entity-Level Authority">
The idea that AI models assess a person, brand, or organization as a unified entity across all indexed content — website, published articles, case studies, third-party mentions, and social profiles — rather than a single page in isolation. It is a working concept in GEO rather than a documented ranking factor: no vendor has confirmed it as a named score.
</DefinitionBox>

## What the Primary Sources Actually Say

Three public sources are worth reading before you trust anyone's list of "signals," mine included.

**Specific, sourced content gets cited more.** The Princeton and Georgia Tech paper [GEO: Generative Engine Optimization](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3) tested content changes on a benchmark of queries. It reports that "our top-performing methods, Cite Sources, Quotation Addition, and Statistics Addition, achieved a relative improvement of 30-40% on the Position-Adjusted Word Count metric and 15-30% on the Subjective Impression metric." It also found that keyword stuffing offers "little to no improvement on generative engine's responses." This is a benchmark study by the authors, not a measurement of ChatGPT's live behavior, but it is the best public evidence that named numbers, quotes, and citations help and that classic keyword tricks do not.

**Google says there is no special trick.** Google's documentation on [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) states: "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." It adds that you do not need "special schema.org structured data" to appear, and points to "creating helpful, reliable, people-first content." For Google's AI features, the foundation is ordinary good SEO on pages that are indexed and eligible to show with a snippet.

**ChatGPT search has a crawler gate.** OpenAI's [bots documentation](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots) says: "Sites that are opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though can still appear as navigational links." If your robots.txt blocks it, nothing else in this article matters for ChatGPT search.

None of these three sources describes an "expertise evaluation" as such. The six signals below are my inference from them, from how the same qualities work on human buyers, and from what I see when I query these tools for my own field. Treat them as a reasoned model, not a leaked rulebook.

## Six Signals AI Models Plausibly Use to Evaluate Expertise

These work together. None is sufficient alone.

### Signal 1: Consistency of claims across publications (inference)

Models draw on many sources, so claims that agree across independent places are easier to repeat with confidence than claims that appear once. My $523 million figure is one claim in one place, but it is also on a [case study page](/case-studies/belron-safelite-523m-campaign) with the client named (Belron), the duration (9 years), and a quote from the client's CEO, Gary Lubner: "You are our secret weapon. Keep your diary clear, please!" That is the pattern worth copying: a claim, a named client, and a third party saying so. Your [case studies](/case-studies) and [portfolio](/portfolio) are not only sales tools. They are the corroboration.

### Signal 2: Specificity of results and claims (supported by the GEO paper)

"I have helped clients increase conversions" is generic. The Dynamic Brain long-form sales page I wrote for Stonehenge Health produced $2.1M in sales in the first 90 days (client-reported, [on my portfolio](/portfolio)). The second one gives a model something to quote and a reader something to check. The GEO paper's finding on statistics and citations is the closest published support for this. It is also the oldest rule in [direct-response copywriting](/blog/what-is-direct-response-copywriting): specificity sells because it is credible.

### Signal 3: Depth of topical coverage (inference)

A copywriter with one article on [sales pages](/blog/how-to-write-a-sales-page) has a thin topical footprint. One with guides on sales pages, [VSLs](/blog/what-is-a-vsl), [email](/blog/how-to-write-email-subject-lines), [landing pages](/blog/landing-page-copywriting), [funnels](/blog/what-is-a-sales-funnel), [conversion copywriting](/blog/conversion-copywriting), [formulas](/blog/copywriting-formulas), and [headlines](/blog/how-to-write-a-headline) covers the subject properly. I infer that retrieval systems favor the second, and the logic is the same one Google applies to "helpful, reliable, people-first content." Random, disconnected posts do not build this; a deliberate architecture with logical internal links does.

### Signal 4: Original insight versus repackaged information (inference)

A model trained on thousands of "what is a sales funnel" articles has no reason to cite the thousand-and-first. What earns a citation is something not found elsewhere: a framework, data, or a lesson from a named campaign. The Belron brief I mention above is an example. Five award-winning copywriters had failed at it before me, and what I learned from it is not available to anyone summarizing other people's articles.

### Signal 5: Named human expertise with verifiable credentials (inference)

Anonymous brand content is easier to ignore than a named author whose claims can be checked. A page that says "written by a copywriting expert" says little. "Written by Rob Palmer, direct-response copywriter for 40+ years, with earlier work for Apple, IBM, and Microsoft and a 9-year Belron campaign that tracked $523 million in sales" can be checked against my [case studies](/case-studies) and [testimonials](/testimonials). If your credentials exist only in your own bio, there is nothing to corroborate them.

### Signal 6: Extractable structure (partly contradicted by Google)

Clear definitions and standalone passages make a page easier to quote, and that is plausible for any retrieval system. But Google says plainly that no special markup is needed for AI Overviews, so do not treat structure as a trick. I cover the formatting mechanics in my [GEO guide for copywriters](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-for-copywriters).

## Genuine Experts vs. Content Farms

Content farms publish high volumes of surface content across unrelated topics, with no named author, generic claims ("experts say," "studies show"), and no first-hand material. A site with 500 articles from pet care to financial planning to copywriting, and no named author, gives a model little to go on, however well each article reads. Genuine experts show the opposite: focused depth, named authorship, specific results, and a body of work that builds on itself. ## The Entity Profile

If the entity idea is right, every place your name appears either strengthens or weakens the picture a model assembles: your site, LinkedIn, guest posts, case studies, testimonials, and third-party mentions. Consistent messaging about one expertise area, specific results repeated across sources, and named client relationships that can be corroborated all help. Contradictory claims, unverifiable credentials, sudden pivots into unrelated topics, thin or outdated content, and anonymous authorship all hurt. This is inference rather than documented behavior, but it matches how a careful human buyer would judge you, which is the safest thing to optimize for.

## Practical Steps

This is the framework I use for my own positioning and when advising clients on [copywriting strategy](/blog/professional-copywriter-services).

### Step 1: Check the gate, then audit your entity profile

Confirm your robots.txt does not block OAI-SearchBot or other search crawlers you want. Then ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews: "Who is [your name]?" and "Can you recommend a [your specialty] copywriter?" Nothing returned means the profile needs building. Inaccurate details are a correction opportunity.

### Step 2: Build topical depth systematically

Map the cluster around your core expertise: [sales pages](/services/sales-page-copywriter), [VSL scripts](/services/vsl-copywriter), [email sequences](/services/email-copywriter), [landing pages](/services/landing-page-copywriter), [sales funnels](/services/sales-funnel-copywriter), and the [verticals](/verticals) you serve. Publish thorough content on each with clear internal links.

### Step 3: Make every claim specific and verifiable

Replace "experienced copywriter" with "40+ years of direct-response copywriting experience." Replace "proven results" with the figure, the client, and the timeframe, as in "$2.1M in sales in the first 90 days." Only claim what you can point to a source for.

### Step 4: Ensure cross-source consistency

Your site bio, LinkedIn summary, guest post bios, and case studies should tell the same story with the same numbers.

### Step 5: Publish original insights

Every piece should contain something a researcher could not extract from existing sources: first-person observations from real campaigns, your own frameworks, lessons from identifiable projects.

### Step 6: Earn external mentions and citations

Guest posts, podcasts, and speaking give independent sources that say what you say about yourself. Each is a corroborating data point.

### Step 7: Structure and maintain the content

Formatting for extraction and keeping pages current are covered in my [GEO guide](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-for-copywriters); I will not repeat them here.

## Why Established Expertise Has an Edge

It is reasonable to expect that a long, consistent, corroborated record helps, and that someone starting today cannot shortcut it. I would not claim to know that AI search rewards incumbency more than classic SEO did; nobody has published evidence either way. What I do know is that the underlying work is the same as it has always been: real results, named clients, and a sustained body of work. For anyone who has spent decades writing copy that had to produce measurable results or get scrapped, that is not a new burden.
