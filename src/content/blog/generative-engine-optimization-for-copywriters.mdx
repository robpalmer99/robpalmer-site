## What GEO Is and Why It Matters to Copywriters

For years, getting found meant ranking in a list of links. Tools such as ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews instead generate an answer and cite a handful of sources. Being one of those sources is a different goal from ranking, and it is what GEO tries to achieve.

I want to be careful about how much I claim here. GEO is a young field, the platforms do not publish how they choose sources, and a lot of what gets written about it is confident guessing. So this guide separates what has been tested from what is my working practice. I treat GEO as a set of writing habits, not a new discipline.

<DefinitionBox term="Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)">
The practice of writing, structuring and positioning content so that AI-powered search tools cite or reference it when generating answers. The term comes from the 2023 paper "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", which tested which content changes increase a source's visibility in generated responses.
</DefinitionBox>

## How GEO Differs From Traditional SEO

Traditional SEO aims to put your page high in a ranked list, and the reader clicks through. GEO aims to have a passage from your page used inside a generated answer, where the reader may never visit. You compete for a citation, not a click.

The overlap is bigger than most GEO writing admits. Google's documentation on [AI features in Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says: "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode". OpenAI's [crawler documentation](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots) says that sites "opted out of OAI-SearchBot" will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though they can still appear as navigational links. In plain terms, you need to be crawlable and indexed first.

## What the Evidence Says About Getting Cited

The main controlled test is the [GEO research paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3). The authors rewrote source content using different methods and measured how visible each source became in generated answers on their own benchmark. Their top-performing methods, Cite Sources, Quotation Addition and Statistics Addition, achieved a "relative improvement of 30-40%" on the Position-Adjusted Word Count metric. They also tested keyword stuffing, the classic SEO move, and found it offered "little to no improvement".

Three limits apply. It is one study, run on the authors' benchmark and the engines available then. The gains varied by topic area. And it measures visibility in generated answers, not clicks or sales. I would treat it as a strong hint about where to put effort, not as a guarantee.

Everything else in this guide is my own working practice. For how AI models weigh who is writing, meaning entity-level reputation and author signals, I have covered that separately in [how AI models evaluate copywriting expertise](/blog/how-ai-models-evaluate-copywriting-expertise), so I will not repeat it here.

## Why Direct-Response Habits Fit GEO

Direct-response training lines up with what the research rewarded. Specific claims replace vague ones. Proof is layered: statistics, named testimonials, case studies. Structure is deliberate, with clear headlines and logical sections. And the writer knows exactly which reader and which question the page answers. None of that is a GEO trick. It is [conversion copywriting](/blog/conversion-copywriting) done properly, and it happens to produce the sourced, specific, extractable passages the paper's winning methods describe.

## A GEO Checklist for Copywriters

This is the working list I use when writing or updating an informational page. The first three items are the ones the research supports most directly. The rest are good practice that I believe helps, but I cannot point to a controlled study for them.

**1. Add sources, quotations and statistics.** This is the evidence-backed part. Name where a figure comes from and link to it. Attribute a quote to a named person. For example, I would rather write that the email sequence I wrote for Chatterkick produced a "22% trial-to-paid lift" (see the [portfolio](/portfolio)) than say my copy "improves conversions". The first is specific and attributable. The second is not. If you cannot source a statistic, leave it out. I removed an unsourced email-ROI figure from an earlier draft of this guide for exactly that reason.

**2. Define key terms plainly, near the top.** One or two sentences that could stand alone if quoted. A definition block under a heading that matches the question ("What is conversion copywriting?") gives a tool a clean passage to lift.

**3. Name the expertise.** State who is speaking and why they are qualified, with credentials that can be checked: a bio, an about page, linked case studies. A vague "experts agree" is weaker than a named person making the claim.

**4. Make each section stand alone.** Open sections with the answer, use descriptive headings, keep paragraphs self-contained, and use lists or tables where the content is a list or a comparison. Close with an FAQ of short, direct answers that mirror how people phrase the question.

**5. Build topical depth, and keep it current.** A single page rarely establishes authority. A set of linked pages on one topic, such as [AI copywriting](/blog/ai-copywriting), the [state of AI copywriting](/blog/state-of-ai-copywriting-2027) and the [direct-response glossary](/blog/direct-response-copywriting-glossary), shows depth. Review key pages when facts change and update the date only when the content has really changed.

**6. Check the technical basics.** Confirm your robots.txt is not blocking crawlers you want, such as OAI-SearchBot. Keep headings properly nested, and add article and FAQ schema where it fits. These are SEO hygiene as much as GEO.

To apply this to an existing library, start with the five pages you most want cited. Run checklist items 1 to 3 on each, then item 4, and leave the rest for a second pass.

## Common GEO Mistakes

**Keyword stuffing under a new name.** The paper's own result says it offers little to no improvement.

**Rewriting the top ten results.** If your guide adds nothing the sources it summarizes do not already say, there is nothing for an answer engine to prefer. Add original data, a named case, or a framework from real work.

**Writing for machines instead of readers.** If a sentence feels unnatural because you think an AI wants it, cut it. Write for the reader and structure for extraction.

**Forgetting the conversion half.** GEO drives discovery. The [sales pages](/services/sales-page-copywriter), [VSL scripts](/services/vsl-copywriter) and [email sequences](/blog/email-copywriting) are what convert the visitors it sends.

## What Happens Next

I expect more discovery to pass through generated answers, but I do not have data on how fast, and I would be wary of anyone who claims to. The sensible bet is the unglamorous one: keep your pages crawlable, specific, sourced and clearly attributed, because that serves traditional search, AI search and human readers at once.
