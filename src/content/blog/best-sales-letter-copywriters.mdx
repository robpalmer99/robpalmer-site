<DefinitionBox term="Sales Letter Copywriter">
A direct-response copywriter who writes long-form persuasion in letter form: printed direct mail packages, and their digital descendants, the text sales letter (TSL) and the long-form sales page. The reader controls the pace, so the letter has to sell to a skimmer through its headline, subheads and bolded lines, and to a reader through the full argument. Sales letter copywriters are judged by one number above all others: how long their letter holds as the control.
</DefinitionBox>

## What a sales letter specialist does that a page writer doesn't

A long-form sales letter is the hardest format to write well and the easiest to write badly, because length hides weakness. Four things separate the specialists.

**They sell to the skimmer and the reader at once.** The headline, the subheads and the bolded lines have to carry the whole argument for the reader who scrolls, while the body rewards the one who stays. Most long copy fails the skimmer.

**They build one belief.** Every winning letter states one belief and spends its length proving it. Letters that try to make five arguments make none. Evaldo's 16-word formula is the test: the new opportunity is the key to the reader's desire, and it's attainable only through this mechanism.

**They attach proof to the objection it answers.** Testimonials gathered in a block get scrolled. Proof placed at the moment of doubt reads as evidence. Specialists know where each doubt arrives.

**They respect the stamp.** Writers who came up in direct mail learned that every letter had to pay for its own postage. That discipline, which demands a reason for every paragraph, is what makes their digital TSLs convert on paid traffic where cheaper copy bleeds.

## The five questions that expose a sales letter copywriter

**"Which letters of yours became controls, and how long did they hold?"** This is the whole interview. Names, numbers, years.

**"Have you written for print as well as the page?"** Direct mail is the sternest test. A writer who has passed it adapts easily to text sales letters.

**"How do you decide what the letter is about?"** You want to hear about research, the one belief, and the gap in the current control, not a headline brainstorm.

**"How do you handle a split test against my control?"** A specialist wants the control, the results and the list, and aims the new letter at what the control fails to do.

**"What would you change about my current letter?"** Send it before the call. A specialist names the belief it fails to build.
