## The Short Answer: Yes and No

Can AI write sales copy? Yes. Can AI write sales copy that reliably converts cold traffic into customers on complex, high-stakes offers? No.

That is my answer after 40+ years of writing [direct-response copy](/blog/what-is-direct-response-copywriting), including a [$523 million tracked campaign](/blog/523m-copywriting-campaign), and after using AI in my own practice every day since 2023.

"Sales copy" covers a vast range. A product description for a $29 kitchen gadget is sales copy. A 45-minute [VSL script](/blog/what-is-a-vsl) for a $997 health supplement running cold Facebook traffic is also sales copy. AI handles the first reasonably well. It fails at the second in ways that cost real revenue.

Marketing and sales content generated by artificial intelligence tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, or specialized platforms like Jasper. AI sales copy ranges from product descriptions and social ads to long-form sales pages and VSL scripts. Its effectiveness varies dramatically by format — performing competently on structured, lower-stakes assets while consistently underperforming on complex, high-stakes conversion assets that require strategic persuasion architecture.

Most of what you will read on this topic comes from people selling AI tools, who say it can do everything, or from threatened copywriters, who say it can do nothing. Both are wrong. This article maps the line for sales copy specifically; for the full workflow, see my guide to [direct response copywriting AI](/blog/ai-copywriting).

## What AI Does Competently

I am not anti-AI. I use [AI copywriting tools](/blog/ai-copywriting-tools) every day. In some categories AI's speed makes it the smarter choice.

### Product descriptions

For standard e-commerce products where the copy needs to be clear, benefit-driven, and SEO-friendly, AI produces serviceable output from specs, keywords, and a tone note. At the scale of hundreds or thousands of SKUs, AI with human quality control beats pure human writing on economics.

### Social media ad copy

Short-form ad copy is constrained by format and character count, and the main skill is volume for testing. AI can produce 30 variations in the time it takes a human to write three. That [testing velocity](/blog/ai-copywriting) matters, because the creative that wins is the one you found fastest.

### Email subject lines

AI is competitive on [email subject lines](/blog/how-to-write-email-subject-lines). In tests I have run, AI-generated subject lines perform within 10% of human-written ones, and the volume means you find winners faster.

### First drafts from detailed briefs

Give AI a sufficiently detailed strategic brief (audience, emotional triggers, persuasion framework, proof, desired outcome) and it produces a first draft that gives a skilled copywriter a head start. "Write a sales page for my supplement" produces garbage. This is the principle behind my [free Claude Code copywriting skills](/blog/claude-code-copywriting-skills), which encode the brief and frameworks into the AI's instructions, including a [direct response copy skill](/blog/claude-code-direct-response-copy-skill).

### Variation generation for testing

Need 50 [headline variations](/blog/headline-formulas) or 20 hook angles for cold traffic? AI delivers them in minutes. This is arguably its single greatest strength in a sales copy context.

## What AI Fails At

There are categories where AI underperforms badly enough that using it alone costs businesses serious money.

### Long-form sales pages

A high-converting [sales page](/blog/how-to-write-a-sales-page) is an engineered sequence: the headline sets a specific expectation, the opening activates a specific emotional state, the mechanism makes the solution credible, the proof lands at the moment the prospect needs conviction, and the offer uses the momentum built up to that point. AI can fill in sections once you tell it what goes where. The decisions about what goes where come from [conversion copywriting](/blog/conversion-copywriting) experience in a specific market. I have seen AI-generated [long-form sales copy](/blog/long-form-sales-copy) test against human-written versions dozens of times, and the human copy wins overwhelmingly, typically by 40-60% on conversion rate.

### VSL scripts

[VSL copywriting](/blog/vsl-copywriting) is the hardest format in direct response. A VSL must hold a cold, skeptical viewer for 15 to 45 minutes while building intensity, credibility, and desire in a precise sequence. AI produces scripts that sound like they should work but lack the rhythm and escalation that keep people watching. In testing, AI-generated scripts underperform human-written ones by 50-70% on both viewer retention and conversion.

### Strategic email sequences

An individual email from a good brief? Fine. A seven-email launch sequence where each email builds on the last, escalates commitment, and meets objections at the right moment? AI treats each email as an independent unit. A great sequence is a connected arc, and that takes [email copywriting](/blog/email-copywriting) expertise.

### Complex offer architecture

Take a hypothetical funnel (an illustration, not a client): a $997 core offer with a $297 bump, a $497 upsell, and a $197 downsell. Each page has to build on the psychology of the previous purchase decision. The upsell copy must reference the emotional state the first purchase created, and the downsell must catch the prospect who refused the upsell without undermining its value. That is [sales funnel architecture](/blog/what-is-a-sales-funnel), and it is judgment built from experience.

### What those decisions look like in practice

The case I know best is Belron. In 1996 the world's largest auto-glass company handed me a direct-response campaign that five award-winning copywriters had already failed at. I ran it for 9 years and it tracked $523 million in sales ([the full case study](/case-studies/belron-safelite-523m-campaign)). More recently, the Dynamic Brain long-form sales page for Stonehenge Health produced $2.1M in sales in the first 90 days (client-reported). The angle was a strategic call made before drafting: name the condition doctors call Mild Cognitive Decline, then make one-ingredient competitors the problem and use a car-service analogy so a 40-compound formula reads as common sense ([see the page excerpt](/portfolio)). Those are the choices that decide whether copy converts, and a model has no way to make them for you.

## Why AI Copy Underperforms on Paid Traffic

Paid traffic is where the truth about copy quality gets exposed, because every visitor costs money and conversion rate decides profitability. AI copy underperforms there for three reasons.

### Cold traffic demands strategic precision

Cold visitors do not know you and are not looking for you. Converting them takes a [persuasion sequence](/blog/aida-copywriting) that meets them at their exact awareness level. AI defaults to generic persuasion, which speaks to everyone and connects with no one, and cold traffic punishes that.

### Every visitor costs money

When the copy is free, as with organic traffic to a blog post, underperformance is annoying. When every click is paid for, it is a direct hit. As an illustration: if one page converts at 1.5% and another at 3%, you pay twice as much per customer on the weaker page. The math rarely favors cheap copy on expensive traffic.

### Competitive markets require differentiation

If your copy sounds like the output of the same tools your competitors use, and AI's default output gravitates toward the statistical average, you have no differentiating message. In a market flooded with [AI slop](/blog/state-of-ai-copywriting-2026), the copy that converts sounds like a human who understands the prospect's specific situation.

## The Gap by Format: What I Have Seen

The figures below are my own observations from testing in markets where revenue was at stake. They are not a published study, and I am not naming clients or campaigns here; the same pattern is covered in my [AI vs human copywriting](/blog/ai-vs-human-copywriting) analysis.

**Long-form sales pages:** AI-only copy converts 40-60% below human-written baseline. The gap is widest on complex offers with sophisticated audiences. The hybrid approach (human strategy, AI-assisted drafting, human refinement) performs 10-20% above the human-only baseline due to faster testing cycles.

**VSL scripts:** AI-only scripts underperform by 50-70% on both viewer retention and conversion. Hybrid approaches improve on pure human performance by 5-15%.

**Email sequences:** AI-only campaigns generate 20-40% less revenue per subscriber than human-written sequences. Individual emails perform closer to parity, but sequence-level performance shows a significant gap.

**Product descriptions:** AI performs within 10-15% of human copy.

**Email subject lines:** AI performs within 10% of human copy, and the volume advantage often tips it in AI's favor.

**Social media ad copy:** AI performs within 15-20% of human copy on individual creative performance, but 30 variations tested against 3 means AI-augmented workflows often find winners faster.

The pattern: the more strategic complexity a format requires, the wider the gap.

## When an AI-Generated Sales Page Is Good Enough

There are legitimate cases where an AI-generated page with some human editing gets the job done.

**Low-ticket products with warm traffic.** If you are selling a $17 ebook to your own email list, the persuasion bar is low: the audience already trusts you and the price is an impulse purchase.

**Offer validation.** Before investing in professional copy, an AI-generated page can test whether people click buy at all. If the offer validates, invest in the real thing. If not, you saved the money on an offer that needed reworking anyway.

**Supporting pages.** FAQ pages, shipping information, and secondary landing pages in your [sales funnel](/blog/what-is-a-sales-funnel) can be produced efficiently with AI and light editing.

## How to Evaluate AI-Generated Sales Copy

Whether the draft comes from a tool or a vendor, check four things.

**Awareness match.** Does the opening match how your traffic arrives? Cold ads need a page that does not assume the reader knows what the product does.

**Emotional specificity.** Does the copy name feelings at a label level ("you feel frustrated") or put the reader in a scene they recognize from their own life? Generic emotions do not convert.

**Proof placement.** A testimonial about product quality belongs near the mechanism, one about customer service near the guarantee, and a case study with numbers where the reader is weighing the price. AI tends to dump all proof into one "testimonials" block because it is following a template, not matching each piece of proof to an objection.

**The skeptic read.** AI copy often feels smooth but unsubstantiated. Read it as someone who does not want to buy and mark every "prove it" or "so what?" Those are the gaps that cost you conversions. If you could swap in a competitor's name and the page still worked, it is not differentiated enough.

## What Businesses Should Actually Do

**If your copy drives paid traffic at scale,** hire an experienced [direct-response copywriter](/blog/what-is-direct-response-copywriting) who integrates AI into their workflow. The fee is usually a fraction of the revenue difference between copy that converts well and copy that converts poorly. To find the right person, start with [how to hire a copywriter](/blog/how-to-hire-a-copywriter).

**If you need volume content,** use AI with human oversight. Product descriptions, social content, and SEO blog articles are where the cost savings justify the small gap.

**If you are somewhere in between,** segment by stakes. Use AI for high-volume, lower-stakes content, and an experienced copywriter for the [sales page](/blog/how-to-write-a-sales-page), the [VSL](/blog/what-is-a-vsl), the launch sequence, and the ads that drive your most expensive traffic.

**If you are using AI-only copy on a high-stakes asset,** test it. Run it against a version written by an experienced human, and measure conversion rate, average order value, and revenue per visitor. The test will tell you what you need to know. For more examples, see [conversion rate optimization case studies](/blog/conversion-rate-optimization-case-studies).

**If you are a copywriter,** develop both strategic depth and AI fluency. Writers whose only skill was assembling competent sentences are the ones struggling; [copywriting is not dead](/blog/is-copywriting-dead), but it rewards depth over volume.

## The Bottom Line

Can AI write sales copy? Yes, for certain definitions of "write" and certain types of "sales copy." Can it write strategic, emotionally precise copy that converts cold traffic on complex offers at a profitable acquisition cost? Not yet.

The businesses getting the best results use both: AI for speed and volume, and experienced copywriters for the decisions where strategic judgment determines whether the copy makes money.
