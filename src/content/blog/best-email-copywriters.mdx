## Ecommerce flows or direct-response sequences?

The two halves of email copywriting look alike and aren't. An ecommerce flow writer builds the automated emails a Shopify brand sends around a product the reader has already looked at: welcome, abandoned cart, post-purchase, win-back. The product does much of the selling and the writer's job is timing, relevance and voice. A direct-response sequence writer builds emails that sell an offer the reader hasn't bought yet: a launch, a VSL follow-up, an affiliate promotion, a nurture into a sales page. The writer does all the selling, and the sequence has to build belief email by email.

Chase Dimond's Structured is the reference for the first job. Most of the rest of this list made their names on the second. I do both, and the question to ask any writer is which one your project is, because a brilliant flow writer can stall on a launch sequence and a launch writer can over-sell a welcome flow.

## The five questions that expose an email copywriter

**"Which sequences have you written, and what did they do in revenue per subscriber or ROAS?"** Open rates are vanity. Revenue is the measure.

**"Show me a sequence arc, not a single email."** You want hook, pain, mechanism, proof, offer, close across the sequence, with each email earning the next.

**"How do you write subject lines for a phone?"** Forty characters or fewer, curiosity or benefit, no spam vocabulary. A writer who hasn't thought about it hasn't tested.

**"How do you handle a cold list?"** Deliverability, relevance, voice. A specialist diagnoses before writing.

**"What would you change about my current welcome sequence?"** Send it before the call. A specialist names the email where readers stop opening.
