## What makes financial copy harder than every other vertical

Two constraints pull in opposite directions, and most writers can only satisfy one.

The audience is the most sceptical in direct response. They have heard every claim, seen every chart pattern, and been burned by enough bad advice to build strong defences against marketing. The copy has to be aggressive enough to get through that.

And every claim has to sit inside the rules. SEC and FINRA for anything regulated, FTC for everything, the publisher's compliance officer, the platform reviewer at Meta or Google. A writer who satisfies the audience and ignores the rules produces a promotion that compliance rewrites into nothing, six weeks late. A writer who satisfies the rules and ignores the audience produces a promotion that reads like a 10-K and converts at half the previous control.

The writers on this list can do both. The test, if you're interviewing someone who isn't on it, is to ask what they do when compliance pushes back on a winning hook. The right answer is that the disclosure was written into the hook before the test, so there's nothing to push back on. I go into the mechanics in my guide to [hiring a financial copywriter](/blog/financial-copywriters).

## What the best financial copywriters charge

Financial publishing pays the highest fees in direct response, because the maths justifies it. A winning promotion for a $1,000 subscription to a 50,000-name list is worth millions, and a weak one leaves most of that on the table.

- **Senior writers with controls behind them:** $15,000 to $40,000 or more per long-form promotion or VSL, often with a royalty of 3 to 5 percent of net sales.
- **Mid-level writers:** $7,500 to $15,000, usually fee only.
- **Agencies:** higher again, with the fee buying a team and a process rather than one named writer.

Royalties started in this vertical and still matter most here, because a financial control can run for years. If a writer offers a reduced fee against a royalty, they're betting on their own work, and that's usually the writer you want.
