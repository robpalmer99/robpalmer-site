## First, Make Sure You Are Hiring for the Right Job

Before you write the job post or the outreach message, get clear on what you are buying.

You are not hiring someone to "make more creative." Your team can already do that. AI can do that. The bottleneck in almost every stalled ad account is not the volume of creative — it is the absence of a system that decides *what* creative to make next and *why*.

That system is the job. A creative strategist develops hypothesis-led concepts, writes the briefs that turn those hypotheses into testable assets, directs production, and reads the performance data to decide the next move. The deliverable is not a stack of ads. It is a testing loop that compounds — where every result, win or lose, teaches the account something and the spend gets smarter over time.

If what you actually need is a single high-stakes asset — a [sales page](/services/sales-page-copywriter), a VSL, an email sequence — then you want a senior [direct response copywriter](/services/direct-response-copywriter), not a creative strategist. If you are running paid acquisition at scale and need someone to own the whole test-and-iterate machine across dozens of variations a week, then a creative strategist is the right hire. Getting this distinction wrong is the single most expensive hiring mistake in the category.

## The One Filter That Separates Real From Fake

Here is the filter I would use if I were hiring one tomorrow.

A creative strategist is really three older jobs fused into one operator. Call them the three brains:

- **Brain 1 — direct response craft.** The persuasion psychology to write a hook that stops the scroll and copy engineered to drive a measurable action. This is the foundation. Without it, the strategist is generating creative on instinct.
- **Brain 2 — AI and tooling fluency.** The ability to direct AI-assisted production so throughput jumps from a handful of concepts a week to dozens — without the output turning to generic slop.
- **Brain 3 — analytical rigour.** The ability to read ad-account data and translate it into the next hypothesis. This is the learning loop. Without it, the spend never compounds into insight.

Almost everyone claiming the title has one brain, maybe two. The one-brain "strategist" who is really just a junior writer. The two-brain operator who can write and analyse but produces at a hand-cranked pace because they never built an AI workflow. Genuine three-brain operators are rare, because the combination requires being equally comfortable in the craft, the tooling, and the numbers — and most people are strong in one and faking the other two.

Your whole job as the person hiring is to find out which brains are real and which are asserted. That is what the next section is for.

## How to Vet a Creative Strategist Before You Hire

Use these in the first conversation. Each one is designed to be hard to fake.

**"Walk me through a test where your hypothesis was wrong. What did you learn?"** A real operator answers this instantly and specifically, because losing tests is how the loop learns. Someone who has never lost a test either never ran tests with enough traffic to detect a loss, or is not telling the truth. Either way, keep looking.

**"Read the last 30 days of my ad data live."** Put a real account in front of them and watch. A three-brain strategist starts spotting patterns, winners, and losers, and forming hypotheses in real time. Someone without Brain 3 will narrate the dashboard back to you and stop there.

**"Show me your AI stack — and what it produces that you cannot produce alone."** Brain 2 is the easiest to fake and the easiest to expose. Make them demonstrate it, not describe it. If the answer is a vague wave at "I use ChatGPT," that is not a workflow.

**"What is your weekly throughput of net-new concepts shipped to test?"** Net-new concepts around distinct hypotheses — not fifteen variations of one idea. An operator with real AI tooling ships 10 to 30 a week. Without tooling, 3 to 5. Under 3 and you are looking at a copywriter, not a strategist.

**"Name three direct response writers whose work you study."** If they cannot name three, Brain 1 is missing. The names should include some combination of Schwartz, Halbert, Hopkins, Caples, Sugarman, Collier, Bencivenga, Carlton, or Georgi. If the only name is a current guru, that is a one-brain answer.

### The red flags

Any one of these is a yellow flag. Three or more and it is a no.

- They talk about creative awards before they talk about CPA. Awards are a brand-world signal; creative strategists are measured on cost per acquisition.
- Their portfolio is hero pieces, not test logs. A real strategist's portfolio includes graveyards — what got killed, why, and what it taught them.
- They cannot name a hypothesis they were wrong about.
- AI fluency is asserted, never demonstrated.
- They have never lost a test. Statistically impossible for anyone who has actually run them.

## Freelance, In-House, or Agency?

Three ways to buy the role, and they are not interchangeable.

**In-house** makes sense when you have consistent, high paid-acquisition spend and want the loop owned full-time inside your team, sitting next to your media buyer every day. The cost is a full salary plus the risk of hiring one person and hoping they have all three brains.

**An agency** makes sense when you need production volume and are comfortable with a layer between you and the person actually doing the strategic thinking. The trade-off is that the senior operator you were sold in the pitch is rarely the one running your account day to day.

**Freelance or fractional** makes sense when you want direct access to a senior operator without carrying a full-time salary — and without an agency layer diluting the work. This is the right shape when you have a stalled account that needs a proven brain on the strategy, not more junior hands on production. It is also, for the record, how I work.

## What It Costs

Pricing varies more than most marketing roles because the supply of real talent is so uneven. Operators with all three brains command the top of every band. Operators with one or two compete on price.

- **In-house salary:** $80,000 to $180,000, and $200K+ with bonuses at high-spend DTC and info-product brands.
- **Freelance day rate:** $800 to $2,500, with the top end reserved for operators who can show you documented test logs.
- **Retainer:** $5,000 to $25,000 per month. This is the most common model, because the role is an ongoing loop rather than a one-off project.

The cheap end of the market is the dangerous end, because the role *looks* doable. Anyone can spin up variations. Few can run a real testing system. A bad hire here does not just waste budget — it teaches your funnel the wrong things, which costs you months of compounding insight you cannot get back.
