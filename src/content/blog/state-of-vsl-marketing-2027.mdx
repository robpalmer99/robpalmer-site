## What Is VSL Marketing, and Where Does It Stand Going Into 2027?

VSL marketing is selling with a video sales letter: a scripted video, usually 8 to 45 minutes long, that takes a viewer from a hook through a problem, a mechanism, proof and an offer to an order button. It's the format behind most scaled supplement, financial and info-product funnels, and that hasn't changed. If you want the full definition and structure, I've written it up in [what a VSL is](/blog/what-is-a-vsl).

Where it stands going into 2027 comes down to one sentence. The format is as strong as ever, the traffic costs more, the tools got cheaper, and the regulators started naming names.

I've written direct-response copy for 40 years, with $523M+ in tracked results across the record, and I still write VSLs for clients. The scripts behind the $40 million Gluco 6 supplement funnel and the $30 million Mobile Profits offer, which reached number one on ClickBank and converted up to 4 percent on cold traffic, are mine. This is my read on the year, with sources where I'm reporting someone else's data and a clear label where the numbers are my own working figures.

## What Changed Since the 2026 Edition?

I published the first edition of this report in February 2026. Here's what moved in the months since, in the order I think it matters to someone running a VSL.

**Traffic got more expensive again.** Meta's [third-quarter 2026 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) report that "average price per ad increased by 14% year-over-year" while impressions grew 10 percent. That figure blends every app, placement and region, so it isn't your CPM. An independent panel points the same way: Adtribute's [benchmark of European DTC brands](https://www.adtribute.io/data/benchmarks/meta-ads-cpm) puts the median Meta CPM up about 10 percent on a year earlier.

**Sora closed.** OpenAI's [help center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001152) states that the Sora web and app experiences were discontinued on April 26, 2026, and the Sora API on September 24, 2026. A year ago plenty of people, me included, expected generated video to reshape VSL production. The tool most of them had in mind no longer exists.

**Avatars and voices went the other way.** HeyGen's [current plan limits](https://www.heygen.com/blog/heygen-monthly-video-duration-limits) allow 30 minutes per video on its Creator plan, which is long enough for a whole short VSL. ElevenLabs lists [Eleven v4](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models.mdx) as its current flagship speech model.

**AI video now gets labeled whether you declare it or not.** YouTube [announced in May 2026](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/) that when a creator doesn't disclose AI use "but our systems detect significant photorealistic AI use, we will now automatically apply a label."

**The FTC took its fake-review rule into enforcement.** In April 2026 the agency [charged the supplement brand TruHeight](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/ftc-takes-action-against-truheight-deceptive-unsubstantiated-advertising-supposed-height-enhancing) with violating the FTC Act and its Reviews and Testimonials Rule.

**Fake urgency drew real fines.** In October 2026 the UK's Competition and Markets Authority [fined three businesses 6 million pounds](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-fines-firms-6-million-for-misleading-sales-tactics), most of it for time-limited offers that weren't time-limited.

**TikTok's US business changed hands.** A US joint venture took over in January 2026, with Oracle, Silver Lake and MGX each holding 15 percent and ByteDance keeping just under 20, according to [TechCrunch's summary of the deal](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/30/heres-whats-you-should-know-about-the-us-tiktok-deal). For a VSL advertiser the practical effect so far is small: TikTok is still a place to find viewers for a hook, and still a poor place to ask a stranger for 15 minutes.

### What I got wrong, and right, last year

I said conversational VSLs, video that adapts to viewer input in real time, would appear within a year. I haven't seen one running at scale, and I'd no longer put a date on it.

I said AI-generated video would make full visual multivariate testing cheap. Partly right. Voice and avatar test cuts are cheap now. Generated footage is still clip-length, and the best-known generator shut down.

I said regulators would tighten on AI content. That one landed, and sooner than I expected. The labeling rules above and the EU's transparency obligations are all in force.

I said fake scarcity was becoming a liability. The CMA has now put a price on it.

## How Long Should a VSL Be in 2027?

Long enough to carry the whole argument and no longer, which for cold social traffic usually means 8 to 20 minutes. That range is my working number from my own projects and reviews. It isn't an industry benchmark, and the companies that host most VSLs don't publish one.

The public data that does exist comes from business video platforms, and it cuts both ways. Vidyard's [video benchmark report](https://www.vidyard.com/video-benchmark/) found that for videos under a minute "65% of viewers stay engaged all the way to the end," while for videos over 20 minutes "only 20% of viewers remain engaged through to the end." Wistia's [2026 State of Video data](https://wistia.com/blog/state-of-video-webinar-recap) found that "a 60+ minute video has a 52% play rate," so people will start a long video if they came for it. Both samples are mostly B2B content watched by people who chose to be there. A cold prospect from a paid ad is a harder audience than either.

Here's how I set length in practice.

**I take the budget from the market's winning controls.** If the three offers scaling hardest in your niche run 12, 14 and 18 minutes, a 40-minute script is a bet against the evidence. I worked on a run of business-opportunity offers during 2026, and the fronts I watched scale in that market mostly ran 10 to 15 minutes.

**I treat short as a density problem.** A lean VSL wins when it keeps everything the long one had: dated, specific proof, a named origin story, an enemy, testimonials that each carry their own result. Cut those and you have a short video that doesn't sell.

**I decide what to lose.** In my experience you can tighten a finished script by about 6 percent through wording alone. Past that, hitting a length target means cutting a section, and it's better to choose which one before you write than after.

**I keep the long version for the people who want it.** A presold email list, a complex financial offer or a high price can all justify 30 minutes or more. The length argument is about cold traffic.

<DefinitionBox term="Short-Form VSL">
A video sales letter of roughly 8 to 20 minutes that carries the full direct-response sequence (hook, problem, mechanism, proof, offer and close) at higher density than the 45-to-60-minute scripts that dominated the 2010s. It is built for cold social traffic and phone viewing. A short-form VSL drops repetition and slow build, and keeps the proof.

## Which VSL Scripting Techniques Are Emerging This Year?

The techniques that separated the scripts I saw win in 2026 from the ones that stalled are mostly about discipline. Six of them are worth stealing.

**One belief, written down before the script.** Before I draft, I write a single sentence in the form Evaldo Albuquerque set out in *The 16-Word Sales Letter*: this new opportunity is the key to the viewer's desire, and it's only attainable through this mechanism. Every section of the script then has to build that belief or go. It sounds basic. It's also the first thing I check when I'm asked to fix a script that isn't converting.

**Proof attached to the objection it answers.** The old structure gathered testimonials into one block after the mechanism. The scripts holding attention now place each piece of proof at the moment the viewer is thinking the objection it answers. Someone doubting the product works at their age hears from a customer their age, right then.

**Proof density matched to the price.** For a low-ticket offer to an audience that has been burned before, I've found a stacked run of proof does more than a careful mechanism lesson. At that price the decision is emotional, and proof speaks to it. Supplements are the opposite case: there, the named mechanism is the sale, and thinning it out costs you.

**A lead that makes the promise.** The hook can be a scene, a confession or an overheard conversation, but inside the first lines the viewer needs to know the problem this solves and that a solution is coming. AI first drafts are weakest at exactly this point, so the lead is where I spend most of my hand-rewriting.

**Emotion on screen.** Flat narration over slides is losing to scripts that give the editor real human moments to cut to: a voice cracking, a customer in tears of relief. I write those moments into the script as events and leave the visual treatment to the video team.

**Written for the ear.** A viewer hears each line once and can't scroll back. So every number carries its unit ("four hundred and forty dollars," never just "four hundred and forty"), nothing depends on the time of day the video happens to be playing, and nothing on the page is unspeakable. Star ratings and bold text don't exist in a voiceover.

One structural rule goes with these. Nothing about price, value, refunds or guarantees appears before the product is revealed. A script that mentions money early tells the viewer they're being sold to before they want what you have.

## What Does Meta's Andromeda System Mean for VSLs?

It means the VSL is the part of your funnel that stays still, and the ads in front of it are the part that has to keep changing.

Andromeda is the retrieval system Meta uses to pick which ads are even considered for each person. Meta's engineers [describe the job](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/) as "selecting ads from tens of millions of ad candidates into a few thousand relevant ad candidates," and write that "the number of ads creatives in Meta's recommendation systems is expected to grow significantly." The same post says more than a million advertisers had used Meta's generative tools to create more than 15 million ads in a month.

Read that as an advertiser and the conclusion is uncomfortable. You're competing inside a system built to handle a flood of creative, against advertisers producing ads by the thousand. One good ad pointed at one good VSL doesn't last.

Three things follow.

**The hook supply becomes a production line.** A proven VSL needs a steady run of new ads: different angles, different opening scenes, different formats, each aimed at the same video. That's the work a [creative strategist](/services/creative-strategist) does, and it's why I now treat the angle map as the first deliverable and the script as the second.

**The ad and the video have to tell one story.** When hooks multiply, it's easy for an ad to promise something the VSL takes four minutes to get to. The viewer who clicked for one thing and gets another leaves. Every new angle needs a matching first thirty seconds, or a lead variation that picks up where that ad left off.

**Conversion rate carries more of the load.** With Meta's price per ad up 14 percent in a year, a VSL that converted well enough last year can be unprofitable on the same traffic now. The script is the cheapest place to find that margin back.

On Meta specifically, a text sales letter often matches or beats a video, because the reader arrives from a scroll and wants to skim. The Abundant text sales letter I wrote reached 340 percent ROAS on Facebook cold traffic without a video on the page. I've set out the traffic-source rule and the rest of the numbers from my own funnels in [VSL vs TSL](/blog/vsl-vs-tsl). Most scaled offers settle it by running both on one page.

## Is YouTube Still the Best Home for a VSL?

For a full-length VSL on cold traffic, YouTube and native are still where I'd start, because the viewer has already chosen to watch something.

The change worth knowing about is the screen. Nielsen reported that in August 2026 YouTube captured "a platform-record 15.8% of TV" viewing in the United States. On Alphabet's third-quarter call, living-room viewing accounted for over a third of YouTube's ad revenue, [as TheWrap reported](https://thewrap.com/industry-news/business/alphabet-earnings-q3-2026/). You can read Nielsen's figures in its [August 2026 report](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2026/tv-viewing-cools-off-in-nielsens-august-gauge-reports-as-sports-take-a-breather-and-school-resumes/).

For years the advice was to design a VSL for a phone, and most of your Meta traffic is still on one. But a YouTube viewer may now be on a sofa with a remote, ten feet from a 55-inch screen, with no way to tap a button under the video. If YouTube is a real share of your traffic, check three things: the on-screen text reads at both sizes, the call to action is spoken as well as shown, and the web address is short enough to type.

## What Can AI Really Do for a VSL Now?

It can research faster, produce hook variations in volume and build a watchable test cut for very little money. It can't yet generate a whole VSL's footage, and it still writes weak leads.

Here's the state of the tools as their own makers describe them.

- **Generated footage is clip-length.** Google's [Veo documentation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo) describes Veo 3.1 as a model for generating 8-second videos with natively generated audio, and notes that Veo output is watermarked with SynthID. That's useful for B-roll and scene inserts. It isn't a way to produce 15 minutes of coherent video.
- **Avatars cover full length.** At 30 minutes per video on HeyGen's Creator plan, a presenter-style test version of a short VSL is a one-day job.
- **Voices are good enough to test with.** I produce test versions of my own scripts through an AI production stack before a client pays for a human shoot. If the script doesn't convert with a synthetic voice and simple visuals, a $25,000 production won't rescue it.
- **Sora is gone.** If your production plan was built around it, it needs a new foundation.

The limits are just as real. Give a model a product and ask for a VSL and you get the shape of one: hook, problem, mechanism, proof, offer, close, each section present and none of them persuasive. The mechanism is borrowed, the proof is vague and the lead could open any video in the niche. I use AI heavily, and I've published [the Claude Code skills I work with](/blog/claude-code-copywriting-skills), but the angle, the one-belief sentence and the first two minutes are still written by hand. My wider view of where AI helps and where it doesn't is in the [State of AI Copywriting](/blog/state-of-ai-copywriting-2026).

### Disclosure is now part of production

Three rules to plan around.

YouTube labels photorealistic AI content automatically when its systems detect it and the creator hasn't declared it. Meta says its ["About this ad" panel](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/) carries "AI info" labels on ads created or significantly edited with its generative tools, and that it will detect ads made with third-party AI tools as well. In the EU, the European Commission's guidance states that ["Article 50 of the AI Act applies as from 2 August 2026"](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act) and that "deployers must disclose deepfake content."

An AI presenter isn't banned by any of this. A VSL that hides one, or that passes a synthetic person off as a real customer or a real doctor, is taking a risk it wasn't taking a year ago.

## What Is Legally Riskier Than It Was a Year Ago?

Fake reviews, undisclosed insiders, fake deadlines and borrowed faces. None of these were safe before. What changed in 2026 is that there are now named cases for each. I'm a copywriter and this isn't legal advice, so take your own script to your own counsel.

**Fake and manufactured reviews.** The FTC's TruHeight complaint alleges the company used fake profiles "to post software-generated comments on TruHeight's Facebook and Instagram pages," and the proposed order requires a $750,000 payment. Before that, in December 2025, FTC staff [sent letters to 10 companies](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-warns-10-companies-about-possible-violations-agencys-new-consumer-review-rule) warning of possible violations of the same rule.

**Testimonials from people who work for you.** In the FTC's case against Growth Cave, a business-opportunity program that the complaint says was "sold via YouTube ads," the agency alleged that the people endorsing the program in its video ads "are all Growth Cave employees—but this is never disclosed." The [January 2026 settlement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/01/ftc-secures-settlement-banning-growth-cave-defendants-marketing-selling-business-opportunities) permanently banned the defendants from selling business opportunities and imposed judgments of $48,597,538.

**Testimonials standing in for evidence.** The FTC's [Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) is blunt on this: "These testimonials don't constitute substantiation." A customer saying a supplement fixed her blood sugar is a claim that the supplement fixes blood sugar, and you need the science to back it.

**Countdown timers that reset.** The CMA found that Wayfair advertised daily and 24-hour deals with countdown timers while "many products remained available at the same price or lower after the stated deadline," and fined it 4.8 million pounds. Five months earlier the mattress company Emma Sleep [admitted breaking consumer law](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/court-endorses-cma-action-as-emma-sleep-agrees-to-change-sales-practices) with misleading countdown timers. The CMA's consumer protection director put it plainly: "using fake countdown clocks or misleading 'discounts' to push people into spending is illegal."

**Deepfaked doctors and celebrities.** The fact-checking group Full Fact [documented AI-altered videos](https://fullfact.org/health/academics-deepfaked-tiktok-wellness-nest/) of real academics and health experts promoting supplements, and one of the people impersonated called his video "a complete fake from beginning to end." Writing in [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/02/17/deepfake-doctors-scam-ama/), the chief executive of the American Medical Association described scammers who "clone the faces and voices of real, credentialed physicians." Google Ads now states that ads impersonating public figures are not allowed "even if they disclose that they were generated or edited using AI," [as PPC Land reported](https://ppc.land/ai-labels-wont-save-public-figure-impersonators-from-google-ads-suspension/) in November 2026.

That last category matters to honest advertisers too. Every deepfake doctor ad makes the next real expert in a real VSL a little harder to believe, and makes the platforms' reviewers a little quicker to reject health creative.

The practical answer hasn't changed in 40 years. Real customers, real results you can document, a real deadline or no deadline, and claims your evidence supports. I write first drafts to sell as hard as the product deserves, and then every script gets a separate compliance pass before it ships. The [compliance checker](https://github.com/robpalmer99/claude-code-copywriting-skills) I use for the first sweep is free in my Claude Code skills.

## Are Interactive VSLs Worth Building Yet?

Only after a straight linear version is already winning.

An interactive or hybrid VSL adds clickable choices and branching, so a viewer who doubts the mechanism can get more of it and a returning viewer can skip to the offer. The idea is sound. In the 2026 edition I quoted lifts of 15 to 30 percent over a linear cut. Those are my working numbers, and I haven't found an independent data set that confirms them, so weigh them accordingly.

What I can say with confidence is where the cost sits. A branching VSL is several scripts that have to stay consistent with each other, every branch needs its own proof, and every test now has more variables in it. If the core argument isn't proven, you've multiplied the work of finding out. Prove the linear script first. Then branch the one section where your retention data shows viewers leaving.

## Where I'd Put a VSL Budget in 2027

If you're planning a new VSL or replacing a tired one, this is the order I'd spend in.

1. **Research and the angle map.** The customer's own words, the competitor scripts, the mechanism, and the list of angles the ads will run on. Everything after this is cheaper if this is right.
2. **The script.** One belief, a length budget from your market, proof placed against objections.
3. **A cheap test cut.** Voiceover and simple visuals, or an AI version. Find out whether the argument converts before you pay for production.
4. **The text version.** Adapt the winning script into a sales letter for the same page, so the skimmers from Meta have somewhere to go.
5. **Hooks, in volume.** New ads every week into the proven video.
6. **Production polish.** Last, and only for the version that already wins.

The common mistake is to spend in the reverse order: production first, hooks as an afterthought, research skipped. If you want numbers for each line, they're in [how much a VSL costs](/blog/how-much-does-a-vsl-cost).
