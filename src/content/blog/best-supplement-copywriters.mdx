## What a supplement specialist does that a general copywriter can't

Three things, and the writer you hire needs all of them.

**A mechanism the buyer hasn't heard.** Supplement buyers are the most sophisticated audience in direct response. They've heard gut bacteria, cortisol, inflammation and blood flow a hundred times each. A specialist researches which mechanisms are exhausted in your niche and builds one that is new to this buyer, which is where the conversion rate lives.

**Claims that sell inside the rules.** A supplement VSL has to make the promise believable without a disease claim, and it has to survive Meta review as well as the FTC. A specialist writes the compliant version first instead of writing the aggressive version and gutting it later, and knows the compliant equivalent for every high-risk move: a routine timeline instead of an outcome timeline, structure-and-function language instead of a cure.

**Funnel economics.** A supplement front end rarely makes money on its own. The profit is in the subscription take rate, the upsell stack and the email back end. A specialist writes the front end knowing what the upsells will carry, and writes the upsells knowing which objection the front end already answered.

If a copywriter's portfolio is one supplement project among twenty others, they may still be good. They haven't lived through enough ad-account rejections to be a specialist.

## The five questions that expose a supplement copywriter

**"Which supplement offers did you write, and what did each do on cold traffic?"** You want conversion rates, spend levels and how long each held as the control.

**"How do you find a mechanism that is new in my niche?"** The answer should involve competitor teardowns and buyer research, not a brainstorm.

**"How do you keep the claims compliant without killing the promise?"** A specialist can name the compliant equivalent for a disease claim, a testimonial outcome, or a timeline. A generalist says "we'll run it past legal".

**"What's in your research process?"** Reviews, forums, support logs, competitor scripts. The hook comes from that layer.

**"What would you change about my current funnel?"** Send it before the call. A specialist comes back with the minute mark, the page section or the upsell where buyers leave.

## What supplement copywriters charge

Senior supplement copywriters with documented results charge $20,000 to $50,000 or more for a VSL or long-form sales page, and many structure the deal as a lower base plus a royalty of 3 to 5 percent of net sales, because supplement funnels at scale produce the revenue that makes a royalty worth having. Mid-level writers charge $7,500 to $20,000. Under $3,000 you're buying a draft with no research in it. The full ranges are in [how much a VSL costs](/blog/how-much-does-a-vsl-cost).
